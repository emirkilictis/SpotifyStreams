Everything here comes from Spotify itself and updates once a day, right after Spotify refreshes its play counts. Numbers on this site are the same numbers you would read off a track page — just kept, compared and totalled for you.

Notifications

iPhone & iPad

Apple only delivers notifications to a site that has been added to the Home Screen. In Safari alone the bell will not work — this is Apple's rule, not something you have set up wrong. It takes about twenty seconds:

  1. Open this site in Safari (Chrome on iPhone cannot do this).
  2. Tap the Share button — the square with an arrow pointing up.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  4. Close Safari and open the site again from the new home screen icon.
  5. Pick your artist, tap Notify me, then Allow.

Android

Nothing to install.

  1. Open the artist you want to follow.
  2. Tap Notify me in the header, then Allow.

Computer

Chrome, Edge and Firefox all work. Safari on Mac needs the site added to the Dock first, the same way iPhone does.

  1. Open the artist you want to follow.
  2. Click Notify me, then Allow.
What you get: one notification per artist per day, sent when that artist's new daily numbers land — not on a timer, so it arrives whenever Spotify actually updates. You follow artists one at a time, so following two means two notifications. Tap Notify me again on any artist to stop.

No bell in the header? Then the browser you are using cannot receive notifications — on iPhone that almost always means the site was opened in Safari instead of from the home screen icon.

About the numbers

Why a song sometimes shows no daily gain

Spotify occasionally stops updating a single track's counter for a few days. The site keeps reading it and shows what Spotify reports, so that song sits at zero until the counter moves again — then the days it missed arrive together.

Why a total can go down

Spotify removes plays it decides were not genuine. When that happens the site follows Spotify rather than keeping a number that no longer exists, and the correction is noted on the song.

Different versions of a song

Remixes, sped-up edits and live versions are counted as their own songs, because on Spotify that is what they are. The same recording appearing on several albums is only counted once.
